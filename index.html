The Why

This project started from a clear problem and a focused goal.

The Problem

Parents and caregivers often struggle to tell a routine cough from one that needs closer attention. When clear information is limited by language or access, that uncertainty grows.

The Goal

Build a simple, privacy-respecting tool that gives clearer acoustic feedback and practical next-step guidance — available in languages that matter to more families, including Amharic, Afaan Oromoo, and Tigrinya.

The Approach

Focus on on-device analysis so raw audio stays private, prioritize multilingual access from the start, and keep the experience calm and usable for stressed caregivers.

How it Works

Three straightforward steps from recording to guidance.

1

Record

Capture a short cough sample using the phone microphone with simple quality feedback.

2

Analyze

On-device processing evaluates pitch, duration, and barking characteristics without uploading audio by default.

3

Guide

Receive a clear likelihood score and practical next-step suggestions based on the results.

Designed with Intention

Every major choice was made to support accessibility, privacy, and real-world usability.

On-Device Analysis

Audio stays on the phone by default. Privacy is treated as a core requirement, not an afterthought.

Multilingual from Day One

Interface and guidance planned for Amharic, Afaan Oromoo, Tigrinya, English, and additional languages.

Calm, Clear Guidance

Results are paired with practical next steps so caregivers are not left with a number and no context.

Symptom Tracking

Support for logging multiple recordings to observe change over hours or days.

Educational Support

Simple explanations of croup and red-flag signs to reduce uncertainty.

Built for Real Conditions

Designed with the understanding that many users may have limited connectivity or be under stress.

About the Project

CroupGuard began as a focused student effort to explore a practical question: can thoughtful technology reduce some of the uncertainty parents face when a child develops a concerning cough, especially in settings where language or access barriers make clear information harder to obtain?

The project prioritizes privacy-first on-device analysis and multilingual access (including Amharic, Afaan Oromoo, and Tigrinya) because these choices directly affect who can use the tool and how safely it can operate.

It is an early-stage prototype built to test ideas, gather feedback, and deepen understanding of both the technical and human sides of the problem. The long-term hope is that tools in this direction can support earlier recognition and more confident decision-making for families who need it most.
